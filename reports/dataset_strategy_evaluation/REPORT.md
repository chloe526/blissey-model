# Blissey Beacon: dataset and training strategy evaluation

Evaluation date: October 17, 2026, America/Los_Angeles. This is an assessment, not a new training run or preprocessing revision.

**Recommendation: conditionally adopt the proposed new experiment for a LA Hacks demo. Use eligible Sessions 01–03 for training, collect a genuinely separate Session 04 for validation, and reserve Sessions 05–06 for final testing. Retire Session 03 as a held-out benchmark for the new model. Preserve the original experiment for comparison.** Do not expect pooling alone to fix the labeling and optimization issues identified below. Do not rebuild the current arrays or launch another job until the collection/evaluation protocol and any label-policy revision are reviewed.

The reason to combine is measured session/participant shift and inadequate positive-event coverage, not simply the number of windows. The resulting model would target the current installation and venue. A clean future holdout can give a fair limited comparison; it cannot establish performance on arbitrary people, rooms or naturally occurring falls.

**What was inspected.** A fresh read of all 96 raw recording directories, including measurements, metadata and available action markers; collector/UI/firmware timestamp and radio code; all three numerical arrays and their original config; both model architectures/trainers/evaluators; remote-data fetching and split guards; saved CNN results and existing-checkpoint CPU inference; and a read-only log snapshot of already-running Veri job `5821d199fa99`, through epoch 3. The public repository at commit `4727901ff595c3a79760c1785f540ec753084dc1` contains only Sessions 01–03. Sessions 04–06 are not available to evaluate yet. No MOMENT weights were downloaded, no new model was fitted, and no existing job was launched, changed or stopped.

Evidence files beside this report are `raw_processed_audit.json`, `model_diagnostics.json`, `moment_log_snapshot.txt`, `source_snapshot.json` and `verification.json`. Raw/source measurement hashes match the current preprocessing manifest. Fresh metadata/annotations match all eligible config entries. Reapplying the exact label and quality rules reproduces every recording's accepted class counts. Six fresh raw windows, one of each class from each split, reconstruct the saved normalized tensors.

Four Session 02 recordings mix 12–17 radio sources and are correctly excluded. Combining sessions does not make them eligible. There are 92 eligible recording trials, 88 with retained windows, three physical collection groups and only two participant IDs. The 18 annotated events are separate annotated trials, not 18 independent participants or environments.

The 49 training fall windows represent ten events, with `[5,5,10,6,3,4,5,5,5,1]` windows per event. Validation's eight fall windows represent four events, with `[2,1,1,4]`; Session 03 has `[7,7,7,0]`. Adjacent windows share 2.5 of their three seconds, or 83.3% of their input duration. Counting these as independent positives substantially overstates evidence and gives the longest accepted event ten times the window contribution of the shortest training event.

Under unchanged eligibility/quality/label rules, pooling gives **2,570 windows: 2,492 normal and 78 fall, representing 17 of 18 annotated events**. Fall prevalence becomes **3.04%**, lower than the current training prevalence. Pooling therefore improves diversity but does not cure class imbalance. It adds only **three usable LA Hacks fall events**. Recompute class weights on the new training set; never balance validation or test by oversampling. If used in a future approved experiment, event/session-aware training weights or sampling can reduce domination by long trials; do not treat oversampled windows as new independent events.

**Participant and environment confounding are substantial.** All training positives are p2; p1 appears in training only as normal. Every validation/test positive is p1. This is confirmed participant-label confounding in the training design. Whether either model actually uses identity cues is a hypothesis, but the design makes it plausible. Pooling supplies p1 positives and weakens that shortcut. It still gives no evidence about an unseen third participant.

Metadata describes Session 01 in the Kennedy Library with nearby people and boards on chairs, Session 02 in a furnished common/community room with couches/table and nearby movement, and Session 03 in the crowded LA Hacks room with boards in cases. Nominal link placement is similar: approximately six feet apart and 1 ft 7 in high. Cases are mentioned in Session 02 too; they are not a proven LA-only change. Generic `room1` labels are reused across locations, so room IDs alone are insufficient. Eligible trials use the same observed primary channel 6 and the same observed signal-mode/bandwidth combinations. These observations confirm different collection contexts; they do not isolate a causal effect of venue, body, crowd, casing or automatic receiver gain.

| 01 | 43.39 Hz | 628 / 1,093 | 57.46% | 17.99 | 9.02 |

There are 185 host gaps over 250 ms in Session 01, 21 in Session 02 including excluded trials, and 215 in Session 03. Only four of these 421 host-gap occurrences have corresponding positive device receive-time gaps below 250 ms. Device timestamps are strictly increasing within eligible recordings, and device-rate medians closely agree with host-rate medians. Most observed long intervals therefore also exist in the device receive timestamps; host buffering alone is not a sufficient explanation. This does not distinguish radio loss from firmware/receiver capture limitations.

The collector assigns one host elapsed time to each serial-read chunk, so identical host times can represent different packets. However, there are only **20 duplicate host timestamps among 129,830 packets** here (0.0154%). Deduplication is not the main data-loss mechanism in this snapshot. Device receive timestamps are available in legacy field 18 and are defined in microseconds; future timing work should validate wrap/reset handling and alignment before changing clocks. Firmware disables Wi-Fi power saving, consistent with the documented timestamp precision condition. [ESP-IDF receive timestamp documentation](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4/esp32/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html).

Current v2 requires 20 unique packets/s, 80% occupancy of 100 ms bins, 90% sample span, samples within 100 ms of each edge and no gap above 250 ms. Session 01's rejected windows include 565 excessive-gap, 272 coverage and 258 edge failures. Session 03 includes 659 gap, 437 coverage and 401 edge failures. These are not the old burst-inflated expected-density calculation. Do not relax the frozen rules to retrieve a particular test fall or increase counts. Assess new capture quality while collecting, without consulting model correctness; retain counts of all attempts and report signal availability and uncovered events.

The amplitude/variation shift also appears within activity categories: empty-room median temporal SD is **4.02 / 1.26 / 2.65** for Sessions 01/02/03. Thus differences are not solely caused by different activity proportions. They may still reflect hardware gain, background motion, multipath, participant effects or capture timing; this dataset cannot separate them causally.

With the current training scaler, global processed means/SDs are **0.00/1.00** for train, **1.02/3.13** for validation and **0.58/1.45** for test. This is confirmed distribution shift, not evidence that validation was normalized with a different formula. The same saved training statistics reconstruct its tensors correctly. MOMENT adds per-window/per-channel RevIN, so global affine scale shift alone cannot explain its failure; temporal/subcarrier-pattern shifts and label/participant confounds remain relevant. CNN GroupNorm is also not a guarantee of cross-room invariance.

**The label policy is conservative but poorly matched to short falls.** Explicit verified normal trials map to 0; a fall window maps to 1 only when all three seconds fit inside the operator interval with an additional 0.25 s margin at both ends. Unpaired/missing fall markers do not become negatives. Host UTC alignment correctly avoids the UI's shifted elapsed origin. These mappings are consistent between preprocessing and both trainers.

However, a fall action interval shorter than **3.5 seconds cannot produce a positive window at all** under this rule; even slightly longer intervals can fail because of the 0.5-second grid. Before quality filtering, the policy excludes **203/216** possible windows from validation fall trials, **491/540** from training fall trials and **188/216** from Session 03 fall trials. Baseline/recovery portions legitimately need exclusion or separate verification, so these counts do not mean all excluded windows should be positive. They do show that the representation is sparse and strongly dependent on interval duration.

Current intervals are approximately 4.05–5.84 s in Session 01, 3.89–8.72 s in Session 02 and 6.94–7.14 s in Session 03. The dataset has only approximate action boundaries, not impact timestamps or synchronized visual proof of the moment of descent. It is unverified whether a given three-second positive contains the main descent or largely post-fall lying. All seven candidate positives from one Session 03 event fail quality, leaving it uncovered.

Fall direction, speed, precise person position and actual impact time are not structured fields in the inspected metadata. Their diversity cannot be established from the `staged_fall` category alone. Record those descriptors prospectively, together with physical block ID, participant, setup/firmware identity and annotation confidence; do not invent them for old recordings.

Before collecting the new protocol, record distinct action onset, descent/impact proxy when observable, end and recovery, with synchronized review if participants consent. A future approved, versioned label rule should identify windows containing a verified fall transition with appropriate context, rather than require an entire three-second window to occur within a short action. Do not prolong actions or label intervals just to make the existing rule accept them. Preserve old annotations and labels. Do not automatically label all baseline/recovery time negative; verify it. The current parser accepts only nine named normal activities; newly named bending, object-drop or chair-motion categories would currently be excluded as unknown, so future ingestion needs an explicit reviewed normal-category mapping.

**Why MOMENT's training improves while validation F1 stays zero.** The retrieved log snapshot provides the following direct evidence:

| Epoch | Training weighted loss | Training fall F1 | Validation unweighted loss | Validation confusion matrix `[[TN,FP],[FN,TP]]` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 4.6002 | 0.3571 | 0.4880 | `[[457,0],[8,0]]` |
| 2 | 2.2069 | 0.6105 | 0.1633 | `[[451,6],[8,0]]` |
| 3 | 1.9076 | 0.6304 | 0.3321 | `[[457,0],[8,0]]` |

This is not a failed pretrained load or a broken backward path. The actual pinned MOMENT-1-large encoder loads, 106,498 head parameters are trainable, 341,240,320 backbone parameters remain frozen, and every epoch performs 835 optimizer updates. It learns some training discriminants while missing every validation positive. Epochs 1 and 3 equal the 98.28% accuracy of always predicting normal. Epoch 2 is not all-normal: it produces six false positives and no true positives. Zero F1 alone would not reveal that distinction.

Confirmed contributors are tiny event support, participant-label confounding, strong acquisition/amplitude/temporal shift, and a high-dimensional concat head (53,248 inputs) trained from ten events. Memorization of session/person cues, inadequacy of frozen pretrained features for these actions, label phase mismatch and excessive optimization steps are plausible explanations, not proven causal diagnoses. First-window logits in the log become extremely confident, but that one normal example does not describe every validation score. Complete train/validation score distributions and per-event outputs would be needed to distinguish calibration failure from poor ranking; they are not logged for MOMENT. No full MOMENT checkpoint was downloaded to this low-space Mac for this evaluation.

The loss implementation itself is correct: `mean(weight[target] * CE_per_example)` uses training-only weights **0.53117 normal / 8.52041 fall** and avoids cancellation by PyTorch's weighted-mean denominator at batch size one. The numerical loss check confirms the intended scaling. [PyTorch cross-entropy definition](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.8/generated/torch.nn.CrossEntropyLoss.html).

There is a separate **optimization concern**: at batch size one, scaling one example's gradient and then clipping its total norm to 1 can substantially cancel that scaling. If both weighted/unweighted gradients exceed the cap, their clipped directions and magnitudes become essentially identical. For a misclassified fall, the weighted two-class bias-gradient norm alone is at least `8.52041 * sqrt(2) * 0.5 = 6.02`, so such an example necessarily invokes the configured clipping. A synthetic gradient calculation reproduces the cancellation without fitting any model. Actual clip frequency and unweighted feature-gradient norms are not logged, so the extent of lost weighting relative to normal examples is unknown. Log per-class unclipped norms and clip frequency before claiming that weighting fully addresses imbalance. Consider a small-batch/gradient-accumulation and learning-rate adjustment only as a bounded development experiment after review. [PyTorch clipping behavior](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.8/generated/torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_.html).

Training's weighted, online, dropout-active metrics are not the same quantity as validation's unweighted, fixed end-of-epoch metrics. A falling training loss and a low validation loss do not demonstrate improved validation recall. While all F1 values are zero, MOMENT's tie-breaker selects lower unweighted validation loss, which is dominated by 457 normal windows. This rule is implemented as configured but offers weak checkpoint selection with only four positive events.

**The CNN has a real ranking/shift problem, not just an unfortunate threshold.** Its fixed saved epoch-1 model has validation matrix `[[209,248],[0,8]]`, fall precision 3.13% and F1 0.0606. Existing-checkpoint CPU inference gives training ROC-AUC **0.924**, validation ROC-AUC **0.636** and validation average precision **0.0245**, compared with validation prevalence 0.0172. These are descriptive window-level summaries with correlated positives, not independent confidence estimates.

Every validation empty-room window (76/76) and lying-still window (61/61) is called fall. The median empty-room fall score is **0.874**, above the median actual-fall score **0.786**. A single score threshold cannot cleanly repair that ordering. In the already-evaluated Session 03 predictions, false positives include 199/199 lie-down windows, 75/75 get-up windows, 185/189 walking windows and 200/221 stand-up windows. These are descriptions of existing predictions, not a test-set threshold search. The saved CNN test matrix remains `[[242,1007],[0,21]]`; three covered events detected does not imply useful discrimination.

CNN weighting is also implemented correctly for batch size one; actual CNN training uses batch size 32. Class-weighted training changes the effective cost/prior, so softmax scores should not be interpreted as calibrated real-world fall probabilities. Both pipelines currently decide at 0.5 (MOMENT training uses equivalent argmax apart from exact ties). A future threshold/alert policy should be fixed using development data and a declared event-recall/false-alarm objective, then frozen before final testing. Do not simply raise or lower it based on Session 03 results. [Threshold selection guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/classification_threshold.html).

**Proposed split and leakage controls.**

| Preferred new experiment | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Sessions 01 + 02 + eligible 03_LAHACKS | Training and training-only scaler/weights |
| New Session 04 | Development: checkpoint selection, any threshold/alert-policy choice |
| New Sessions 05 + 06 | Final evaluation after all choices are locked; report each session separately and pooled |

This is technically reasonable once enough new labeled events exist. Target-domain training is plausibly useful for deployment at that installation because measured collection distributions differ, consistent with broader Wi-Fi sensing evidence about cross-environment/person shift. Improvement is not guaranteed, especially with only three existing LA fall events. [FewSense primary research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02014).

Do not arbitrarily reserve a few old overlapping windows as a new test: all old sessions have been examined and Session 03's performance has already informed discussion. Use all eligible old recordings for the new training version while preserving the old experiment. Do not relabel, relax quality for, or add mixed-source recordings. Record that Session 03 is intentionally promoted from historical test to training; its old scores are no longer an unbiased evaluation of the new model.

A session name does not by itself create independence. Sessions 04–06 should be physically separate collection blocks with a genuine time break, independently repeated actions and documented crowd/setup conditions. Keep every recording, fall event and all of its overlapping windows in one split. Long continuous recordings also stay in one role. Shared people and a fixed installation can be appropriate for a venue-specific demo but do not demonstrate participant- or room-independent generalization. If a new person is reserved for final testing, every one of that person's recordings must stay out of training and development for that particular claim. [Grouped evaluation principles](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html).

If Session 04 exposes failures requiring additional labeled LA training examples, a valid contingency is **train 01–04, validate 05, test 06**. Make that promotion explicit before inspecting model results on 05/06, refit training-only transforms, and freeze the final-test role of 06. This gives up a second independent test block and leaves a weaker final evaluation. Prefer an additional dedicated adaptation block when time permits, so two final sessions remain untouched. Do not train on 04 and continue calling it validation.

**Normalization and code changes needed later, not performed here.** The existing 52 physical LLTF carriers, three-second context, 512-point grid and fixed quality-v2 gates can support a pooled experiment. Upsampling still adds no temporal information. Refit one scaler on the newly declared training records only and recompute class/event weights. Never fit a separate whole-validation/test-session scaler or average statistics across final holdouts. MOMENT's fixed per-window RevIN uses each input window and is distinct from fitting statistics on the test dataset.

Keep the original arrays, scaler, source revision, checkpoints and old results. Publish a separately named split/data version with explicit split/annotation/quality versions and hashes. Regenerate from verified raw data under the agreed prospective label rule; do not merely concatenate normalized arrays and retain the old Session-02-only normalization metadata. With unchanged labeling/quality, concatenation of existing tensors shares one old scaler, but it does not accomplish the necessary new train-only refit or establish the new split provenance.

`preprocess.py` currently deliberately refuses any collection-group list other than the original three and hardcodes their assignments. It will not implement this proposal automatically. Later implementation needs an explicit split manifest, globally disjoint group checks and training-only feature/scaler fitting. MOMENT's loader checks separation among requested splits only; add a manifest-wide train/validation/test group-overlap check without loading test tensors. CNN already validates all declared recording groups. Current data are actually disjoint; this is a future guard improvement, not an observed leak.

`evaluate.py` currently insists the entire preprocessing dictionary equal the checkpoint's dictionary, so the old baseline cannot simply be pointed at a new holdout manifest. A reviewed evaluation adapter should preserve each model's saved channel/scaler transform, verify compatible quality/label semantics and attach the new recording provenance without silently bypassing integrity checks. The current MOMENT event evaluator is test-specific and does not provide validation event metrics for checkpoint selection. Future operational model selection needs development event outputs and normal monitored exposure. Existing false-alarm calculations cover only evaluated normal trials and exclude acquisition outages; report uptime and all uncovered falls alongside conditional model recall.

Collect spare attempts to cover acquisition/annotation failures; apply quality criteria before model scoring and retain all attempted-event/availability counts. Do not discard a technically valid final-test attempt because the classifier misses it. Thirty extra overlapping windows are not thirty events. The plan adds 10 development events and 20 final-test events, a substantial improvement over the present four-event validation split. If resources fall below this floor, describe the outcome as a small pilot and avoid extensive model/threshold tuning. Even 20/20 detections has an exact two-sided 95% binomial lower recall bound of about **83.2%**, assuming independent events; shared people/sessions make that assumption optimistic. [Small-sample interval guidance](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm).

Use **at least three participants in total if feasible; aim for four**, with participants contributing both positive and negative activities. The existing participants should contribute across activities rather than recreate the p2-fall/p1-normal confound. Include a new participant; a further final-only participant can provide a small explicitly labeled exploratory unseen-person subset. If only p1/p2 are available, test new collection blocks honestly and make no new-person claim. Target usable trials, not exhausting repeated falls for a quota.

Prioritize normal actions that the current classifier confuses with falls: intentional lying down, getting up from the floor, walking across/near the link and quick but controlled sit/stand transitions. Also include empty-room/background periods, sitting/lying still and nearby bystander movement. Add correctly annotated bending/picking up objects, crouching/kneeling, chair movement and objects being set down where they reflect the demo environment; explicitly map any new normal labels before future ingestion. Interleave categories instead of collecting all normals first and all falls after a setup/crowd change. Keep positive and negative trials under matching positions, people and background conditions.

Keep the **same ESP32 pair, firmware, cases, fixed AP source filter, primary channel, nominal bandwidth, board heights/orientations, transmit behavior and serial settings** throughout the core demo experiment. Current capture settings use `ANY`; the observed multi-source failures justify pinning the intended source for future collection. Log actual receive rate, gap/coverage diagnostics, RSSI and device timestamps. Vary person position/orientation and moderate background conditions before varying the radio installation itself. Broad deployment robustness would require a separate multi-placement study.

Only use consenting participants and supervised controlled simulations with appropriate padding; do not request uncontrolled impacts or unfamiliar fast/backward falls to fill a grid. Where safely feasible, vary comfortable descent speeds, orientations and positions along/near/off the line between devices, with the action timing verified separately. If safe falling cannot be arranged, prioritize hard negatives and describe any positive data as controlled simulations. Do not alter the action speed to satisfy the current minimum-duration labeling artifact.

At least half of final normal exposure should include uninterrupted ordinary/background activity rather than only isolated scripted clips. False-alarm claims need time: zero false alarms over 20 minutes still admits an approximately **9/hour** one-sided 95% upper rate under a stationary Poisson assumption. A short hackathon recording cannot establish a low deployment alarm rate. Count false-alert episodes, event recall, detection availability and approximate latency honestly; precise impact latency needs appropriate timestamps.

**Model priority and fair comparison.** Run a new CNN baseline first after the data/protocol are approved: it has 21,666 parameters, trained in about three seconds locally in the previous run and is cheap to inspect. Do not assume the old CNN is already usable; its current false-positive rate is unacceptable. Retain MOMENT-1-large frozen-head fine-tuning as a second, bounded comparison if time/approved budget allow. The current GPU log measures roughly 7.5 minutes per old-data epoch; this is materially different from CNN iteration time. There is no evidence here to justify unfreezing hundreds of millions of MOMENT parameters on 17 represented events.

Freeze the original CNN checkpoint and the original MOMENT run's predeclared checkpoint-selection rule. Evaluate old and new checkpoints on the **same untouched new final recordings**, with each checkpoint's saved preprocessing. Compare event hits/misses and normal alert episodes on matched recordings, and report results by session/person as well as pooled totals. Do not compare new-model accuracy on new sessions directly with old-model accuracy on Session 03 and call the difference an improvement. Never run the original model on final sessions early and use its errors to change collection, thresholds or the new model.

For new experiments, predeclare a common operational development objective, such as maximum covered-event recall subject to an explicit false-alert budget, with a deterministic tie-breaker. Freeze threshold, alert merging/cooldown and checkpoint choice before final evaluation; if nothing meets the budget, report that failure. Keep window F1/PR-AUC, unweighted loss, per-event counts and signal coverage as supporting diagnostics. Training-only class weights, realistic continuous normal exposure and hard negatives address different aspects of the problem; no single threshold replaces them.

For a **hackathon demo**, reusing inspected Sessions 01–03 for supervised venue adaptation is reasonable and should be disclosed. For **unbiased research**, retain the historical experiment and present this as a new prospectively defined evaluation, with untouched future tests and clear subject/session/venue limits. Once final-test results have influenced another change, those sessions become development evidence and a fresh final holdout is required for the revised system.

The next decision is to approve the prospective collection/split/annotation protocol, not to submit training immediately. Current code, arrays, labels, model weights and settings remain unchanged; verification is recorded separately.
